J-Link BASE and servo wiring for the RedBoard Artemis Nano

Flying-lead wiring with no SparkFun 20→10 adapter. Every pin on the Nano side was read from SparkFun's Eagle schematic (RedBoard-Artemis-Nano.sch), not from forum posts.

1What connects where

Only two signals need the small 1.27 mm debug footprint: SWDIO and SWDCK. They aren't on the 0.1" headers. Reference voltage, reset and ground are on the normal 0.1" header, so jumper wires work for those.

2Connector faces

Getting wires onto a 1.27 mm footprint. The holes are plated through, so pick one of these:
  • Simplest: solder two short 30 AWG wires into holes 2 and 4, with a 0.1" female pin on the other end to plug into the J-Link.
  • Reusable: solder a 2×5 1.27 mm male header, then use 1.27 mm→2.54 mm female–female jumpers.
  • No header: test pads TP1 (SWDCK) and TP2 (SWDIO) are the same nets. Tack wires there.
Keep the SWD leads under about 15 cm. If J-Link can't connect reliably, drop the speed to 1 MHz (-speed 1000).

3Check it with J-Link Commander

Plug the Nano into USB-C first. VTref only senses the board's voltage; it doesn't power the board.

JLink.exe -device AMA3B1KK-KBR -if SWD -speed 1000 -autoconnect 1
mem32 0x10000, 4

Expected result: Found SW-DP with ID 0x2BA01477, Cortex-M4 identified. The read at 0x10000 returns the app's vector table (initial SP, reset vector). Don't run erase. It wipes the SparkFun SVL bootloader at 0xC000 and you lose USB flashing.

4Servo: FEETECH FS0307

The servo gets its own 5 V supply. The Nano only provides the control signal and a shared ground. The signal pin is D9 (Artemis pad 39, marked PWM-capable). Phase 2 confirms the timer (CTIMER) segment that drives that pad in Zephyr. If it doesn't fit, any other PWM-capable pin works.
